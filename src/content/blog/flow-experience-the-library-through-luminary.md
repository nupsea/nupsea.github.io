This is the experience I want reading with Luminary to feel like — not a feature list, but a day in the life: a trip to the library. The story runs on top; the italic caption under each screenshot says what you are actually looking at. What changed since my last post is at the bottom.

## Determination

You've been procrastinating about reading. Your super-talented friend inspires you. Today, you decide to take up a membership at the state library.

> *Some of the best things in life are free.*

Your membership is a download: grab the macOS bundle from the [releases page](https://github.com/nupsea/luminary/releases) and drag Luminary into Applications. You've taken the first step toward reading. You set a reading time and put together a timetable.

## The Road Ahead

It's time to head to the library. You open the app, and your friend is right there beside you as you follow the green, twisting pathway across the park.

*The launch screen: "Opening your library", with a line about how learning works.*

You're greeted by huge wooden doors opening onto a magnificent campus. As you step in, a quote carved into a stone wall catches your eye.

> *The unexamined life is not worth living.*
> — Socrates

<img src="/blog/flow-experience-the-library-through-luminary/asset3.png" alt="Luminary home screen with a Socrates quote, the daily flashcard review and books to pick back up" style="display:block;max-width:480px;width:100%;margin:1.5rem auto;border-radius:0.5rem;" />

*The home screen: a quote of the day, your daily flashcard review, and the books and notes to pick back up.*

You linger on the thought, momentarily lost in it, until a message on your phone breaks the spell — your partner, asking you to meet for lunch. You reply, put your phone on silent, and make a mental note to set a timer. Looking up, you take in the sheer abundance of books before you. You don't know where to start. You settle on a genre, climb the winding stairs to level two, and head toward the section that interests you.

## Dig Deep

A particular topic still isn't clicking, even after re-reading. You turn to your expert friend and select the passage, and he elaborates in plain language.

<img src="/blog/flow-experience-the-library-through-luminary/asset8.png" alt="A selected paragraph about HNSW with an Explain button, and a plain-language explanation beside it" style="display:block;max-width:800px;width:100%;margin:1.5rem auto;border-radius:0.5rem;" />

*Select text and choose Explain (or Ask, or a highlight colour). The explanation stays inside the passage: where the book does not detail the parameters, it says so instead of inventing them.*

You discuss it further; your friend offers an analogy, and when you push with a deeper question, he points you back to the book itself.

<img src="/blog/flow-experience-the-library-through-luminary/asset9.png" alt="The Ask AI panel answering the question How is HNSW implemented, beside the page it came from" style="display:block;max-width:800px;width:100%;margin:1.5rem auto;border-radius:0.5rem;" />

*Ask AI: a chat beside the page, answering from your material. The model picker at the top lets you choose a local model or a hosted one with your own key.*

## Recall

Your reminder nudges you softly. (The focus timer ends.) It's about time to leave and meet your waiting partner. You let your friend know it's time to go and gather your things — glad you nearly made it to the end of the chapter. Your friend checks in: how well did you really understand the topic? Feeling fairly confident, you ask to be quizzed. On the way to the restaurant, the questions come — some easy, some that make you think. Before each answer is revealed, you're asked to predict how you'll do. Your neurons start firing, forging stronger connections.

<img src="/blog/flow-experience-the-library-through-luminary/asset10.png" alt="The Map: a graph of the concepts found across the library, linked where they appear together" style="display:block;max-width:480px;width:100%;margin:1.5rem auto;border-radius:0.5rem;" />

*The Map: the concepts Luminary found across your library, linked where they show up together.*

Then comes the honest part. Luminary compares what you predicted with how you actually did, and it is not always flattering: 28% of my predictions matched over 18 graded ones. Fairly confident, less right than I thought — but now you know exactly where you stand.

<img src="/blog/flow-experience-the-library-through-luminary/asset11.png" alt="The Progress page: prediction calibration at 28 percent over 18 graded predictions, plus streak, level and XP earned" style="display:block;max-width:480px;width:100%;margin:1.5rem auto;border-radius:0.5rem;" />

*Progress: prediction calibration (how often your guess matched your grade), then streak, level and XP, which track showing up, not what you have learned.*

## Sense of Satisfaction

Caught up in the discussion, you barely notice arriving — until you spot your partner smiling in the distance. She greets you both, but your friend excuses himself and says goodbye. *(Luminary closes. Your friend was Luminary all along: the guide who explained, answered from the book itself, and quizzed you on the way. You shut the laptop, and your place and your notes are kept for next time.)*

As you settle in with your partner for lunch, you have a relaxed, enjoyable meal — laughing, talking, sharing. Just as you're about to pay the bill, your partner notices a card fallen a couple of feet from the table. She picks it up and hands it to you with a smile. You take a look, and a similar smile spreads across your face.

<img src="/blog/flow-experience-the-library-through-luminary/asset12.png" alt="The Luminary About card: version 0.12.11, public mode, and a note that your documents, notes and review history stay on your machine" style="float:right;max-width:220px;width:100%;margin:0.25rem 0 1.25rem 1.5rem;border-radius:0.5rem;" />

*About: "Your documents, notes, and review history stay on your machine. Everything lives in a local .luminary folder — copy it to back up or move to a new machine."*

It was never a lending library. Everything you read, wrote and were quizzed on lives in one folder that is yours.

That's the flow I'm building toward — not another chat window bolted onto your files, but a library experience, built around retrieval you can actually trust, grounded in the books you brought with you. This release gets us a step closer.

## What's new since my last post

My last post on Luminary was at 0.8.28. This walk is 0.12.11, so here is what changed in between.

- **A library that looks like a library.** Books and papers show their covers, videos their thumbnails, and every item carries three labels — form, domain and register — that you can filter on.
- **Your work sits beside the page, not over it.** Notes, the conversation, Explain, the flashcard generator and the teach-back session are now faces of one docked panel next to the reader, instead of five pop-up dialogs.
- **Citations land on the passage.** A citation points in the terms its source has: a moment for a recording, a page for a paginated document, otherwise the section. It opens with the passage marked and centred. Since 0.12.11, that holds for every citation you click, not only the first.
- **Fast or private, and a receipt for either.** The first run asks where answers should come from, each option with its measured latency, and no key still means a fully working local app. Every answer carries a receipt: engine, latency, cost, and what left your machine.
- **Practice is a recall loop.** The answer isn't shown until you commit to one, and revealing it takes the reader to the section the card came from. A teach-back is scored against a rubric and can be answered again, and finished sessions can be reviewed card by card with your predictions next to what you actually recalled.
- **Dictate instead of typing** into a note, a teach-back answer or a question. Speech-to-text runs on your machine and is installed as an optional component.
- **It tells you what your machine can run.** Luminary checks whether your machine can run a local model and says so; on one that can't, local model calls are refused rather than attempted.
- **More ways in.** O'Reilly Learning books ingest from their URL for subscribers, EPUB figures and covers now render, and Key Points and Detailed summaries are assembled from the section summaries without an LLM call.

The full list is in the [changelog](https://github.com/nupsea/luminary/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md).
