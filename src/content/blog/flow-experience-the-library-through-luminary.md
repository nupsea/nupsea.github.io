## Determination

You've been procrastinating about reading. Your super-talented friend inspires you. Today, you decide to take up a membership at the state library.

> *Some of the best things in life are free.*

Your membership is a download: the macOS bundle, dragged into Applications. You've taken the first step toward reading. You set a reading time and put together a timetable.

It's time to head to the library. You open the app, and your friend is right there beside you as you follow the green, twisting pathway across the park.

You're greeted by huge wooden doors opening onto a magnificent campus. As you step in, a quote carved into a stone wall catches your eye.

> *The unexamined life is not worth living.*
> — Socrates

<img src="/blog/flow-experience-the-library-through-luminary/asset3.png" alt="Luminary home screen with a Socrates quote, the daily flashcard review and books to pick back up" style="display:block;max-width:480px;width:100%;margin:1.5rem auto;border-radius:0.5rem;" />

You linger on the thought, momentarily lost in it, until a message on your phone breaks the spell — your partner, asking you to meet for lunch. You reply, put your phone on silent, and make a mental note to set a timer. Looking up, you take in the sheer abundance of books before you. You don't know where to start. You settle on a genre, climb the winding stairs to level two, and head toward the section that interests you.

## Recall

Your reminder nudges you softly. (The focus timer ends.) It's about time to leave and meet your waiting partner. You let your friend know it's time to go and gather your things — glad you nearly made it to the end of the chapter. Your friend checks in: how well did you really understand the topic? Feeling fairly confident, you ask to be quizzed. On the way to the restaurant, the questions come — some easy, some that make you think. Before each answer is revealed, you're asked to predict how you'll do. Your neurons start firing, forging stronger connections.

<img src="/blog/flow-experience-the-library-through-luminary/asset10.png" alt="The Map: a graph of the concepts found across the library, linked where they appear together" style="display:block;max-width:480px;width:100%;margin:1.5rem auto;border-radius:0.5rem;" />

## Sense of Satisfaction

Caught up in the discussion, you barely notice arriving — until you spot your partner smiling in the distance. She greets you both, but your friend excuses himself and says goodbye. *(The laptop closes, and Luminary with it. Your friend was Luminary all along.)*

As you settle in with your partner for lunch, you have a relaxed, enjoyable meal — laughing, talking, sharing. Just as you're about to pay the bill, your partner notices a card fallen a couple of feet from the table. She picks it up and hands it to you with a smile. You take a look, and a similar smile spreads across your face.

That's the flow I'm building toward — not another chat window bolted onto your files, but a library experience, built around retrieval you can actually trust, grounded in the books you brought with you. This release gets us a step closer.

## What's new since my last post

My last post was at 0.8.28; this walk is 0.12.11.

- **A library that looks like one:** covers and thumbnails, and filters by form, domain and register.
- **One docked panel beside the page** for notes, chat, Explain, cards and teach-back, instead of five dialogs.
- **Citations open on the passage,** and every click works, not only the first.
- **Fast or private, with a receipt** for every answer: engine, latency, cost, and what left your machine.
- **Practice hides the answer until you commit;** teach-back is scored and can be retried.
- **Dictation, O'Reilly URL ingest and EPUB figures.**

The full list is in the [changelog](https://github.com/nupsea/luminary/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md).
