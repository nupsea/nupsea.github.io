Regrets are sparks of fire that pile up, waiting to be cleared by showers of gratitude. The question to ask is:
> Have we thanked enough for what we have received?

We have been bestowed with plenty: been cared for, nurtured, fed, educated, and instilled with gems of wisdom. Being on the receiving end, did we ever pay back our debts of gratitude, or even think of giving back? How often do we even consider it, or does the thought only hit us once those godly figures have left us? Such is the ungrateful state of human beings, showing up all on its own.

So the remorse goes: *“This was one huge missed ‘Thank you for all that you’ve done!’”*

I remember the effortless comfort of childhood: hot meals waiting before hunger even struck, lunch lovingly brought to school, and a home filled with warmth and healthy choices. When friends weren't around, family stepped in seamlessly, turning regular evenings into pure fun with carrom, antakshari, cricket matches, and movies.

Just as steady was the not so quiet guidance. It was an environment of subtle discipline, curious questions to test our learning, and timeless life lessons passed down naturally—all built on thousands of little, everyday sacrifices we only truly appreciate in hindsight.

I dearly miss that warmth of love. The memories keep pulling me into a bottomless sea—hard to swim out and come up for air. Maybe that’s what sorrow is: a whirlpool. It is what we are left to bear—carrying the burden all along for our ingratitude.

How do we proceed from here?

> Know your karma. It truly belongs to us.

Our actions are driven by our thoughts, and our thoughts are shaped by our environment and state of mind—our energy, health, connections, knowledge, and past experiences. What drove us yesterday might not drive us today, and what didn’t move us then might move us now.

> Inculcate gratitude. Direct it inward first, then outward.

I am deeply grateful to have known you and to have shared such a meaningful part of my life with you. Where would I even be without you?

I am so glad my kids had the chance to play with you and feel that same warmth.

Most of all, I take comfort knowing that you will always remain with me — my **north star**!
